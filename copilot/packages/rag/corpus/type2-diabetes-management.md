## Glycemic Targets

For most non-pregnant adults with type 2 diabetes, a reasonable HbA1c target is
**below 7%**. The target should be **individualized**: a tighter goal (e.g. 6.0–6.5%)
may suit younger patients with long life expectancy and no significant cardiovascular
disease if achieved without hypoglycemia, while a looser goal (e.g. 7.5–8.5%) is
appropriate for older patients, those with limited life expectancy, advanced
complications, extensive comorbidity, or a history of severe hypoglycemia. Avoid the
harms of overtreatment: an HbA1c persistently **below 6.0%** on agents that cause
hypoglycemia warrants de-intensification.

## Treatment Intensification

Reassess glycemic control approximately **every 3 months** while therapy is being
adjusted. **Intensify pharmacologic therapy when the HbA1c remains above the patient's
individualized target** despite adherence and lifestyle measures. Intensification
options include adding a second agent (e.g. a GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2
inhibitor, which also offer cardiovascular and renal benefit in appropriate patients),
a sulfonylurea, or basal insulin. Weigh hypoglycemia risk, weight effects, renal
function, cardiovascular status, and cost when selecting an add-on.

## First-Line Pharmacologic Therapy

**Metformin is the preferred first-line agent** for type 2 diabetes unless
contraindicated or not tolerated, because of its efficacy, low hypoglycemia risk,
weight neutrality, and cost. **Renal dosing caution:** do **not initiate** metformin
when the estimated GFR is **below 45 mL/min/1.73m²**, and do **not use** metformin when
the eGFR is **below 30**. If a patient is already on metformin and the eGFR falls to
**30–45**, continue with caution at a reduced dose and monitor renal function; stop
metformin if eGFR falls below 30 or during acute illness that threatens renal
perfusion.

## Statin Therapy in Diabetes

The USPSTF recommends a **statin for the primary prevention of cardiovascular disease
in adults aged 40 to 75 years who have one or more cardiovascular risk factors**
(dyslipidemia, diabetes, hypertension, or smoking) **and an estimated 10-year CVD risk
of 10% or greater**. Diabetes is itself a major risk factor; most adults with diabetes
in this age range warrant at least **moderate-intensity statin therapy**. Reinforce
adherence and address an elevated LDL cholesterol in a patient with diabetes already on
a statin before assuming the regimen is adequate.

## Blood Pressure Targets in Diabetes

Hypertension is common in type 2 diabetes and compounds cardiovascular and renal risk.
A commonly used treatment target is a blood pressure **below 140/90 mmHg**, with a lower
goal (e.g. below 130/80) considered for selected patients when it can be achieved safely.
An **ACE inhibitor or ARB is preferred** first-line in patients with diabetes and
albuminuria or reduced renal function, for renal protection.
