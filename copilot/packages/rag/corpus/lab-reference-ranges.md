# Adult Laboratory Reference Ranges — Primary Care Reference

Curated from public-domain sources: MedlinePlus (NIH) Medical Encyclopedia, the American
Diabetes Association (ADA), the ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline, and KDIGO / CKD-EPI. These are
**general adult reference ranges**, not patient findings; a specific laboratory's cutoffs and the
individual clinical context always take precedence. Ranges vary with age and sex where noted.

## How to read a value against a range

A value inside the typical range is not automatically "normal" for a given patient, and a value
outside it is not automatically abnormal — interpret against the patient's age, sex, medications,
and trend over time. Absence of a result is "not on file," never "normal."

## Hematology

### Ferritin (iron stores)
Ferritin depends strongly on sex and, in women, on menopausal status:

- Premenopausal women: **13–150 ng/mL**.
- Postmenopausal women: values rise toward the male range (**30–400 ng/mL**); after menopause the
  monthly iron loss of menstruation stops.
- Men: **30–400 ng/mL**.

Low ferritin is the earliest marker of iron-deficiency anemia; a low-normal ferritin with a low
MCV and falling hemoglobin suggests iron deficiency. Source: MedlinePlus (NIH); the pre-/post-
menopausal distinction reflects hematology practice (American Society of Hematology).

### Hemoglobin
- Women: **12.1–15.1 g/dL**.
- Men: **13.8–17.2 g/dL**.

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH).

### Mean corpuscular volume (MCV)
- Adults: **79–95 fL** (sex-neutral).

A low MCV (microcytosis) accompanies iron-deficiency anemia; a high MCV (macrocytosis) accompanies
B12/folate deficiency. Source: MedlinePlus (NIH).

## Metabolic and renal

### Hemoglobin A1c
- Nondiabetic adults: **4.0–5.6 %**. 5.7–6.4 % indicates prediabetes; ≥ 6.5 % is diagnostic of
  diabetes.
- For a patient managing diabetes, the usual treatment target is **< 7 %** (individualized).

Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) / American Diabetes Association.

### Fasting glucose
- Fasting adults: **70–99 mg/dL**. 100–125 mg/dL indicates prediabetes; ≥ 126 mg/dL (fasting, on
  two occasions) is diagnostic of diabetes. Source: MedlinePlus (NIH) / ADA.

### Serum creatinine
- Women: **0.5–0.95 mg/dL**.
- Men: **0.7–1.3 mg/dL**.

Women's lower range reflects lower average muscle mass. Source: MedlinePlus (NIH).

### Estimated GFR (eGFR)
- **≥ 60 mL/min/1.73 m²** is considered normal kidney function; a sustained value below 60 for
  three months or more defines chronic kidney disease. Source: KDIGO / CKD-EPI.

### Potassium
- Adults: **3.5–5.0 mmol/L**. Source: MedlinePlus (NIH).

## Lipids (desirable levels for cardiovascular risk)

- Total cholesterol: **desirable < 200 mg/dL**.
- LDL cholesterol: **optimal < 100 mg/dL**.
- HDL cholesterol: desirable **≥ 40 mg/dL in men**, **≥ 50 mg/dL in women** (higher is protective).
- Triglycerides: **normal < 150 mg/dL**.

Source: ACC/AHA cholesterol guideline.

## Thyroid

### TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone)
- Adults: **0.4–4.0 mIU/L**. The upper limit rises modestly with age. Source: MedlinePlus (NIH).
